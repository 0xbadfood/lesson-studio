# Figures for "Closing a hole between the heart's upper chambers" (topic-asd-device-closure)

Audience: parents, no biology background. Level: figure_class 10 (clear, labelled, not textbook-dense).
Style: see-through child's heart, front view (the child's right is the viewer's left), oxygen-poor blood blue,
oxygen-rich blood red, device metal grey with a fabric patch inside. Every part named below is a separate,
lightable group with the id given (`part-...`), so the narration can light it.

Sources referred to (short names as in digest.json):
- NCERT = NCERT Class 11 Biology Ch 15 (kebo115.pdf)
- AKH = SickKids AboutKidsHealth, ASD closure in the heart catheterization lab
- SCHN = Sydney Children's Hospitals Network, ASD transcatheter device closure factsheet
- BHF = British Heart Foundation, Atrial septal defect
- CINCI = Cincinnati Children's, Atrial Septal Defects
- JUNG = Jung and Choi, J Thorac Dis 2018 (PMC6174149)
- DALLAS = Children's Health Dallas, septal defect closure
- LEEDS = Leeds Teaching Hospitals, ASD keyhole closure leaflet

## 1. `heart-chambers` (shared with other topics)

A see-through four-chamber child's heart. Shared figure: stages `normal`, `asd`, `vsd` (and others added by
other topics). This film uses `normal` and `asd`.

Parts (all stages):
- `part-right-atrium`, `part-left-atrium` (smaller upper chambers), `part-right-ventricle`, `part-left-ventricle`
  (larger lower chambers; ventricle walls clearly thicker than atrial walls). NCERT: "two relatively small upper
  chambers called atria and two larger lower chambers called ventricles"; "the walls of ventricles are much thicker
  than that of the atria".
- `part-atrial-septum`: thin muscular wall between the two atria. NCERT ("A thin, muscular wall called the
  interatrial septum").
- `part-ventricular-septum`: thick wall between the ventricles. NCERT ("a thick-walled, the inter-ventricular septum").
- `part-vena-cava` (superior and inferior, entering the right atrium), `part-pulmonary-veins` (entering the left
  atrium), `part-pulmonary-artery` (leaving the right ventricle, to the lungs), `part-aorta` (leaving the left
  ventricle, to the body). NCERT (blood from the vena cava and pulmonary veins enters the right and left atria; the
  right ventricle pumps into the pulmonary artery, the left into the aorta).
- `part-tricuspid-valve`, `part-mitral-valve` (between atrium and ventricle on each side), `part-pulmonary-valve`,
  `part-aortic-valve`. NCERT. Valves: blood flows one way only.
- Optional thin arrows `part-flow-blue` (body -> right atrium -> right ventricle -> lungs) and `part-flow-red`
  (lungs -> left atrium -> left ventricle -> body).

Stage `normal`: atrial septum intact; blue and red flows stay separate.

Stage `asd`:
- `part-defect`: a hole in the MIDDLE of the atrial septum (secundum ASD: BHF "the hole is in the middle of the
  atrial septum. It is the most common type"). Not at the lower edge near the valves (that is a primum defect) and
  not up at the vein entries (sinus venosus) - BHF.
- `part-rim`: the ring of septal wall left around the hole, drawn as a visible margin all round (SCHN: "the hole
  needs enough tissue around it for the device to stay in place"; JUNG on rims).
- `part-shunt-flow`: a red/purple arrow from the left atrium THROUGH the hole into the right atrium, then on into
  the right ventricle and pulmonary artery, drawn thicker than normal to show extra blood to the lungs (AKH:
  "oxygen-rich blood to leak from the left side of the heart to the right side ... more blood than necessary is
  flowing through the right ventricle to the lungs"; CINCI: "mostly from the left atrium to the right atrium").
- Optional: right atrium and right ventricle drawn slightly enlarged (SCHN: right side "work harder and can make
  it enlarged").

## 2. `catheter-entry` (shared with other topics)

The child lying on their back, a simplified outline of the body from chest to thigh, with the heart and the big
vessels from the groin to the heart drawn see-through.

Stages and parts:
1. `groin`: close-up of the top of the leg. `part-femoral-vein` (blue, on the inner side, towards the middle of the
   body) and `part-femoral-artery` (red, just outside the vein). `part-skin-site` a small mark where the tube goes
   in. AKH: "a vein or artery in your child's neck or groin. The groin is the area at the top of the leg"; BHF: "a
   large vein, usually at the top of the leg". (Vein medial to artery is standard groin anatomy, not stated in the
   digest's sources; the narration does not name it.)
2. `sheath`: `part-sheath`, a short plastic tube with a valve end sitting in the femoral vein at the skin.
   (Generic catheter-lab equipment; the ASD sources say only "a small tube (catheter) is placed in a blood vessel
   in the leg (groin)" - SCHN.)
3. `route-vein`: `part-catheter` running from the groin up the femoral vein, `part-inferior-vena-cava` up the
   abdomen, into `part-right-atrium`. For ASD the catheter then crosses the hole into the left atrium (see figure
   3). CINCI: "directed to the heart through the large blood vessels in the groin"; NCERT: the vena cava empties into
   the right atrium.
4. `route-artery` (used by the VSD film and others, not this film): catheter up the femoral artery and aorta.
- `part-xray` (optional): an X-ray camera arc above the child, and `part-toe-probe`: a thin ultrasound probe in the
  food pipe behind the heart (child asleep). AKH, SCHN (TOE in the oesophagus, X-ray "fluoroscopy").
- `part-ice-probe` (optional): a tiny ultrasound probe passed up the vein into the right atrium - JUNG
  (intracardiac echocardiography "gradually replacing" TOE in some centres).

## 3. `asd-occluder-steps`

Close-up of the atrial septum seen from the front, cut through, right atrium on the viewer's left, left atrium on
the right; the hole in the middle of the septum. Steps in order (each a stage):

1. `measure`: `part-catheter` comes up from the inferior vena cava into the right atrium and through the hole;
   `part-sizing-balloon` (a small soft balloon on the catheter tip) filled inside the hole, its waist pinched by the
   rim. AKH: "a catheter with a small deflated balloon on the tip ... The balloon is inflated to measure the size of
   the hole again." (JUNG: some centres skip balloon sizing when the rims are good; the narration does not need it.)
2. `device-folded`: `part-delivery-catheter` across the hole, tip in the left atrium; the device folded long and
   thin inside it. AKH: "the discs are folded so the device will fit in the catheter".
3. `left-disc-opens`: `part-occluder-left-disc` pushed out and opened flat in the LEFT atrium, then pulled back
   against the left side of the septum. AKH: "one disc opens up as the device is moved out of the catheter";
   JUNG speaks of the "left atrial (LA) disk" and of it prolapsing "into the right atrium before proper
   positioning", i.e. it is opened in the left atrium first.
4. `waist-in-hole`: `part-occluder-waist`, the short tube between the discs, filling the hole. AKH: "The tube
   portion plugs the hole".
5. `right-disc-opens`: `part-occluder-right-disc` opens on the RIGHT atrium side; the septum rim is held between
   the two discs like a sandwich. AKH: "the other disc opens up on the opposite side of the hole". Discs are of
   different sizes (AKH: "different-sized discs"); keep both larger than the hole so they overlap the rim
   (`part-rim`).
6. `check`: device still screwed to `part-delivery-cable`; a TOE probe icon and an X-ray icon; small check mark.
   SCHN: "The device is checked with TOE and X-ray before it is released."
7. `release`: cable unscrewed and withdrawn; device alone in the septum; no shunt arrow any more. SCHN: "Once in
   place, the device opens and closes the hole securely".
8. `healed` (months later): a thin pink layer of the heart's lining grown over both discs; label "within six
   months". DALLAS: "The lining of the heart wall will seal over the device within six months"; BHF: "become
   covered over by your own tissue".

Device appearance: a self-expanding double disc of fine metal wire mesh (nitinol) with a central waist, with a
fabric (polyester) patch sewn inside the discs and waist (JUNG, Amplatzer Septal Occluder: "self-expanding
double-disk device with central connecting waist composed of nitinol-wire-mesh. The disks and waist were sewn with
a Dacron patch"). Draw a generic device: no brand name, no logo.

Not to draw: erosion or a device falling out (the film states these risks in words only, LEEDS and AKH; no
alarming imagery).
